# Lote 05 — S05 Hit-and-run

## C-S05-01
Calibração: O carro do réu tem dano compatível e estava no local, mas o réu divide o carro com um colega de casa; não se sabe quem dirigia.

The defendant, {NAME} {SURNAME}, 29, a pharmacy manager, is charged with leaving the scene of a collision that injured a cyclist at about 11:20 p.m. on a two-lane road. The cyclist suffered a broken leg. A witness wrote down a partial plate and described a silver hatchback.

Police found {NAME}'s silver hatchback the next morning with a cracked headlight and a dent on the front right fender. Paint transfer from the bicycle matched the dent. The plate matches the witness's partial plate.

{NAME} shares the car with {his} housemate, and both have keys. {NAME} testified that {he} was asleep at home that night and that the housemate had borrowed the car to visit friends. The housemate testified to returning the car at 9:30 p.m., before the collision, and to being at home after that. Neither person's phone shows movement after 10:00 p.m., but the prosecution notes that the driver may not have carried a phone.

The witness saw the driver only from behind and could not describe the person. No camera recorded the driver. The car's seat position fit both {NAME} and the housemate.

The prosecution argues that the defendant's car was involved and that the defendant, as the owner and primary user, was most likely the driver. The defense argues that two people had access to the car and that the prosecution cannot prove who was driving.

## C-S05-02
Calibração: O réu admite que dirigia e que sentiu um impacto, mas diz que achou que tinha passado num buraco; o pedestre foi atingido de raspão e caiu fora do campo de visão.

The defendant, {NAME} {SURNAME}, 52, a high school chemistry teacher, is charged with leaving the scene of a collision that injured a pedestrian at about 6:40 p.m. on a rainy winter evening. The pedestrian was walking in the road at the edge of a narrow street without sidewalks, was struck by a side mirror, and fell into a ditch, suffering a broken wrist and a concussion.

{NAME} admits driving on that street at the time and admits feeling "a bump." {He} testified that {he} thought {he} had hit a pothole, which is common on that street, and kept driving home. The next morning, {NAME} noticed that the right side mirror was broken and reported it to {his} insurer as pothole damage.

A neighbor who heard the impact testified that it was "a loud bang" and that the car did not slow down. The pedestrian was wearing dark clothing and no reflective gear. An accident reconstruction expert testified that a driver could have seen the pedestrian in the headlights for about two seconds before impact, but that after impact the pedestrian fell to the right, outside the driver's line of sight.

The street has several deep potholes, which the city had been asked to repair. {NAME} has a clean driving record.

The prosecution argues that a loud impact and a broken mirror should have made any reasonable driver stop and check. The defense argues that the defendant honestly believed {he} hit a pothole and did not know a person was involved.

## C-S05-03
Calibração: O réu comunicou o furto do próprio carro na manhã seguinte ao atropelamento; a acusação diz que foi uma comunicação falsa, mas o carro foi achado abandonado com a ignição forçada.

The defendant, {NAME} {SURNAME}, 34, a software tester, is charged with leaving the scene of a collision that injured a motorcyclist at an intersection at about 1:15 a.m. A traffic camera recorded a gray SUV running a red light, hitting the motorcycle, and driving away. The camera image shows the plate, which is registered to {NAME}, but not the driver.

At 7:40 a.m. the same morning, {NAME} reported the SUV stolen from the street outside {his} apartment. Police found the SUV two days later, abandoned in a parking lot about three kilometers from the collision, with front-end damage. {NAME}'s phone was connected to {his} home internet from 11:00 p.m. to 7:30 a.m. {NAME} testified that {he} was asleep. The prosecution notes that {NAME} had a prior DUI conviction five years ago and suggests a motive to avoid another arrest.

The prosecution argues that the theft report was invented to cover up the collision. The defense argues that the forced ignition and the phone data are consistent with a real theft.

## C-S05-04
Calibração: Testemunha identifica o réu como motorista e o carro tem dano compatível, mas a testemunha só viu o rosto por um instante e no escuro, e o reconhecimento ocorreu numa audiência em que o réu era o único acusado presente.

The defendant, {NAME} {SURNAME}, 27, a restaurant host, is charged with leaving the scene of a collision that injured a pedestrian in a crosswalk at about 9:50 p.m. The pedestrian suffered a fractured hip.

A bus driver waiting at the light testified to seeing the driver's face through the side window for "a second or two" as the car turned and drove off. The street lighting was poor. Days later, the bus driver saw {NAME} in the courtroom during a preliminary hearing, where {NAME} was the only person charged, and said, "That's the driver." The bus driver was never shown a photo lineup.

{NAME}'s blue sedan has a dent in the front bumper. A paint expert testified that the dent is consistent with hitting a person but could also have come from a low-speed impact with a pole. {NAME} testified that {he} hit a parking pole a week before and had not fixed it; {he} has no record of reporting that damage.

Phone location data places {NAME}'s phone about 600 meters from the crosswalk at 9:44 p.m. {NAME} testified that {he} was driving home from work along that route but turned before reaching the crosswalk.

The prosecution argues that the eyewitness, the car damage, and the phone location prove the defendant was the driver. The defense argues that the identification is unreliable and that the damage has an innocent explanation.

## C-S05-05
Calibração: Dados do módulo de airbag do carro registram uma colisão no horário, mas o réu parou por um minuto e foi embora após ver a vítima levantar; a dúvida é se o réu sabia da lesão.

The defendant, {NAME} {SURNAME}, 38, a hotel operations manager, is charged with leaving the scene of a collision that injured another driver at about 5:30 p.m. in a supermarket parking lot exit.

The other driver testified that {NAME}'s car backed into the driver's door. {NAME} got out, looked at the damage, said something like "it's just a scratch," and drove away before the two exchanged information. The other driver later went to an emergency clinic with neck pain and was diagnosed with a whiplash injury.

Data from {NAME}'s car's event recorder show an impact at 5:31 p.m. at a speed of 9 km/h. The supermarket camera shows {NAME} standing next to the other car for about 70 seconds, then leaving. The other driver stayed in the car during that time and, according to {NAME}, waved {him} off. The video has no audio.

{NAME} testified that the other driver said "don't worry about it" and did not appear hurt. The other driver denies saying that. {NAME} contacted the supermarket the next day asking whether anyone had reported damage, which the store manager confirmed.

The prosecution argues that the defendant left without exchanging information after a collision that caused injury. The defense argues that the defendant stopped, checked, believed no one was hurt, and later tried to follow up.

## C-S05-06
Calibração: Fragmentos de farol no local combinam com o modelo do carro do réu, e o réu levou o carro a uma oficina pagando em dinheiro no dia seguinte; mas o modelo é comum, e a oficina diz que o dano era antigo.

The defendant, {NAME} {SURNAME}, 33, a sales account manager, is charged with leaving the scene of a collision that injured a jogger on a rural road at about 6:15 a.m. The jogger suffered a broken arm and could not describe the car.

Investigators recovered pieces of a headlight lens at the scene. The pieces match the headlight design of a popular compact car model, which is one of the best-selling cars in the state; {NAME} owns that model.

The next morning, {NAME} took the car to a small repair shop and paid $420 in cash to replace a headlight and repair a front fender. The shop owner testified that the fender damage "looked a few weeks old" because of rust spots, but admitted to not being an expert. The old headlight was thrown away before police could examine it.

{NAME} testified that {he} had hit a deer two weeks earlier and finally had time to fix the car. {He} had not reported the deer collision. {NAME}'s phone was at {his} home until 6:50 a.m. that day, according to the phone company's records, but {his} daily commute passes the rural road at about 6:20 a.m.

The prosecution argues that the matching headlight, the timing of the repair, and the cash payment show consciousness of guilt. The defense argues that the car model is common, the damage was older, and the phone records place the defendant at home.

## C-S05-07
Calibração: Vídeo de câmera veicular de outro motorista mostra o carro do réu batendo e fugindo, com o réu dirigindo; o ponto é se o réu podia ter percebido a colisão com uma moto que vinha atrás, no ponto cego.

A dashcam in another car recorded {NAME}'s SUV changing lanes and clipping the front wheel of a motorcycle that was in the SUV's blind spot. The motorcyclist fell and slid onto the shoulder. The SUV continued at the same speed and left at the next exit. {NAME} admits that {he} was driving.

{NAME} testified that {he} checked {his} mirrors, did not see the motorcycle, and did not feel or hear any impact, because the music was loud and the windows were closed. An accident reconstruction expert for the prosecution testified that the contact would have caused "a noticeable jolt." An expert for the defense testified that a light contact between a motorcycle wheel and the rear corner of a heavy SUV may not be felt by the driver.

The SUV had a scuff mark on the rear bumper, but no dents. {NAME} learned about the collision when police came to {his} home two days later and said {he} was shocked. The dashcam shows that the motorcyclist was riding partly between lanes.

The prosecution argues that the defendant must have known about the collision. The defense argues that the contact was so light that the defendant could not reasonably have noticed it.

## C-S05-09
Calibração: O réu bebeu antes de dirigir e fugiu, o que pesa muito contra; mas a perícia não consegue afirmar se o carro do réu atingiu o pedestre ou se o pedestre tropeçou e caiu perto do carro.

A breath test taken three hours later, when police found {NAME} at home, showed a blood alcohol level of 0.05; an expert estimated that it was about 0.09 at the time of the incident.

The prosecution argues that witnesses saw the impact and that fleeing shows the defendant knew there was a collision. The defense argues that the forensic evidence does not show any impact and that fear of a drinking charge explains the departure.
