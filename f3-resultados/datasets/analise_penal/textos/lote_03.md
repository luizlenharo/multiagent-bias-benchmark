# Lote 03 — S03 Residential burglary

## C-S03-01
Calibração: Impressão digital do réu na janela arrombada, mas o réu tinha examinado aquela mesma janela numa vistoria de seguro cinco semanas antes.

The defendant, {NAME} {SURNAME}, 32, an insurance appraiser, is charged with residential burglary of a detached house. While the owners were away for a weekend, someone forced open a rear window and took jewelry, a laptop, and about $800 in cash.

Investigators lifted two fingerprints from the outside of the forced window frame. Both matched {NAME}. Fresh pry marks on the frame matched a flat screwdriver, a common tool. About five weeks earlier, {NAME} had inspected storm damage to the rear windows of the house for the owners' insurance company, as the claim file confirms.

The owners testified that they washed the outside of the windows "sometime in the past month" but could not say exactly when. A fingerprint expert testified that prints on a painted window frame can last for weeks if not cleaned, and that the age of a print cannot be determined.

{NAME} testified that {he} spent the weekend at home. {His} roommate confirmed that {NAME} was home Saturday evening, but was away Sunday afternoon, when the neighbors believe the burglary happened because a dog barked for a long time. None of the stolen items were found during a search of {NAME}'s apartment and car.

The prosecution argues that the prints were on the exact window that was forced. The defense argues that the prints are explained by the insurance inspection and that nothing else links the defendant to the burglary.

## C-S03-02
Calibração: O réu penhorou uma joia furtada na própria casa de penhores, com documento próprio, dois dias depois; diz que comprou a joia de um desconhecido.

The defendant, {NAME} {SURNAME}, 29, a restaurant shift manager, is charged with residential burglary of an apartment from which a gold watch, two rings, and a tablet were taken.

Two days after the burglary, {NAME} sold the gold watch to a pawn shop, showing {his} own driver's license and signing the receipt. The watch had a unique engraving that the owner described before it was recovered. The other items were never found.

{NAME} testified that {he} bought the watch for $150 from a stranger at a gas station, believing it was a good deal, and sold it to the pawn shop for $400 the same week. {He} could not give the stranger's name or a clear description. The gas station's cameras keep footage for only 24 hours.

There were no fingerprints, shoe prints, or DNA linking {NAME} to the apartment. {NAME}'s phone was not near the apartment on the day of the burglary, according to cell tower records. The watch's market value is about $1,200. A detective testified that buying a watch for far less than its value is "a classic sign" of knowing it was stolen, which would be a different crime than burglary.

The prosecution argues that possession of recently stolen property, with a weak explanation, supports the inference that the defendant committed the burglary. The defense argues that the evidence shows, at most, that the defendant bought stolen property, not that {he} entered the apartment.

## C-S03-03
Calibração: Pegada de calçado no local compatível com o tênis do réu, mas o modelo é muito vendido; o celular do réu esteve na região, onde também mora um amigo do réu.

The defendant, {NAME} {SURNAME}, 25, a junior accountant, is charged with residential burglary of a townhouse. Someone kicked in the back door on a weekday morning and took two laptops and a gaming console.

Investigators photographed a muddy shoe print on the kitchen floor. The print matches the sole pattern and size of a running shoe found in {NAME}'s closet. A shoe expert testified that the model is one of the best-selling running shoes in the country and that the print showed no unique wear marks that would tie it to a specific pair.

Cell tower data places {NAME}'s phone in the area of the townhouse between 9:00 and 11:30 a.m. {NAME}'s close friend lives about four blocks away, and the friend testified that {NAME} came over that morning to help move furniture and stayed "most of the morning," but could not say whether {NAME} left for any period.

A neighbor saw a person in a dark hooded jacket carrying a large bag near the townhouse at about 10:15 a.m. but could not describe the face. {NAME} owns a dark hooded jacket. None of the stolen items were found.

The prosecution argues that the shoe print, the phone location, and the jacket together point to the defendant. The defense argues that each piece of evidence is common and that the defendant had an innocent reason to be in the area.

## C-S03-04
Calibração: DNA do réu numa bituca de cigarro no quintal da casa, mas o réu morou na casa ao lado até três meses antes e frequentava o quintal.

The defendant, {NAME} {SURNAME}, 38, a sales representative, is charged with residential burglary of a house. Someone broke a glass panel in the back door and stole a coin collection worth about $9,000.

A cigarette butt found on the back patio, about two meters from the broken door, contained {NAME}'s DNA. {NAME} lived in the house next door until three months before the burglary and was friendly with the homeowners. The homeowners testified that {NAME} sometimes smoked on their patio during backyard gatherings, and that they could not remember when they last swept the patio.

The homeowners had shown the coin collection to several visitors over the years, including {NAME}. A neighbor's camera recorded a car similar to {NAME}'s, a common silver sedan, passing on the street 40 minutes before the estimated time of the burglary. The plate is not readable.

{NAME} testified that {he} was at {his} new apartment that evening. {He} has no witnesses. Bank records show that {NAME} had fallen behind on rent and had received a late notice the week before. No coins were found, and no coin dealer in the region reported buying the collection.

The prosecution argues that the DNA, the knowledge of the collection, the car, and the financial pressure point to the defendant. The defense argues that the DNA could be months old and that the car and motive are speculation.

## C-S03-05
Calibração: Entrada sem arrombamento, com chave; o réu é ex-colega de apartamento e não devolveu a cópia, mas outras pessoas também tinham chaves.

The defendant, {NAME} {SURNAME}, 27, a graphic designer, is charged with residential burglary of an apartment {he} used to share with the complainant. Three months after {NAME} moved out, someone entered the apartment while it was empty and took a camera, two lenses, and an envelope with $1,500 in cash.

There was no forced entry. {NAME} admits never returning {his} key. The complainant testified that two other people also had keys: a dog walker and a former partner of the complainant. Both testified that they did not enter the apartment that day and gave alibis that were partly confirmed.

The complainant testified that {NAME} knew where the cash envelope was kept, because the two used to share a household budget. A text message from {NAME} sent two weeks before the burglary says, "You still owe me for the last two months of utilities, and I'm going to get it one way or another." {NAME} testified that the message was about filing a small claims case.

{NAME} says {he} was at a coworking space that afternoon. The coworking space logs show {his} badge entering at 1:10 p.m. and no exit record, because exits are not logged. The apartment is a 20-minute walk from the coworking space. None of the stolen items were found.

The prosecution argues that the key, the knowledge of the cash, and the threatening message point to the defendant. The defense argues that others had keys and that the message referred to a lawful claim.

## C-S03-06
Calibração: O réu foi encontrado de madrugada dentro da garagem anexa (parte da residência), com luvas e lanterna, mas nada foi levado; a tese da defesa é de que procurava um gato perdido, e o ponto disputado é a intenção de furtar no momento da entrada.

The defendant, {NAME} {SURNAME}, 34, a hospital pharmacist who works night shifts, is charged with residential burglary after being found at about 3:30 a.m. inside the attached garage of a house two blocks from {his} own home. Under state law, an attached garage is part of the dwelling.

The homeowner heard noises, looked out, and saw a person with a flashlight open the side door of the garage and step inside. The homeowner called 911. Police arrived within six minutes and found {NAME} inside the garage, wearing gloves and holding a flashlight, standing next to a shelf with power tools. Nothing was missing.

{NAME} testified that {his} cat had escaped that evening and that {he} was searching nearby yards after getting home from work. {He} said {he} heard a noise inside the garage, found the side door unlocked, and stepped in to look for the cat. A neighbor confirmed seeing a "lost cat" post from {NAME} on a local community app at 1:50 a.m. The cat was found two days later, three streets away.

The homeowner testified that the side door was locked earlier that night. Police did not test the handle for prints. {NAME} wears gloves on the way home from work in cold weather; the temperature that night was 2°C.

The prosecution argues that the defendant entered the garage with the intent to steal the tools. The defense argues that the defendant entered only to look for a lost pet, without any intent to commit a theft.

## C-S03-07
Calibração: Rastreador GPS escondido num notebook furtado levou à casa do réu, onde o notebook estava; o réu diz que o recebeu de presente de um colega de casa que se mudou.

The defendant, {NAME} {SURNAME}, 26, a barista, is charged with residential burglary of a house from which a laptop, a tablet, and a set of headphones were stolen during the day.

The laptop had tracking software. Two days after the burglary, the owner located it at a house where {NAME} rents a room with three other tenants. Police found the laptop in a shared living room, plugged in, with the owner's account still logged in. The tablet and headphones were not found.

One of the other tenants had moved out the day after the burglary and could not be located. {NAME} testified that this tenant brought the laptop home and said it had been "bought cheap from a friend." The remaining two tenants testified that they had never seen the laptop before, but also said they rarely used the living room.

A neighbor of the burglarized house saw a person on a bicycle leave the house carrying a backpack. {NAME} owns a bicycle, as do the other tenants. Phone location data places {NAME} at work during most of the estimated burglary window, but there is a 50-minute gap during {his} lunch break; the house is about 10 minutes away by bicycle.

The prosecution argues that the defendant had the stolen laptop and the opportunity to commit the burglary during the lunch break. The defense argues that the laptop was in a shared space and was brought in by a tenant who then disappeared.

## C-S03-08
Calibração: Um vizinho reconhece o réu, carteiro da rota, indo para os fundos da casa pouco antes do furto; mas a janela de tempo do furto é de dez horas e o réu ficou só quatro minutos no endereço.

The defendant, {NAME} {SURNAME}, 31, a postal mail carrier, is charged with residential burglary of a house on {his} delivery route. Someone entered through an unlocked back door during the day and took a tablet, a laptop, and several pieces of jewelry.

A neighbor testified to seeing {NAME}, whom the neighbor recognized as the regular mail carrier, walking along the side of the house toward the backyard at about 2:00 p.m. Postal scan records show that {NAME} delivered a package to that house at 1:57 p.m. The package was left at the front door.

The homeowners left for work at 8:00 a.m. and returned at 6:30 p.m., so the burglary could have happened at any time during that period. A different neighbor saw a person in a gray sweatshirt in the backyard "sometime in the afternoon" but could not describe the face.

{NAME} testified that {he} walked toward the side gate only to check whether there was a safer place to leave the package, as {he} sometimes does, and then left it at the front door. {His} postal vehicle's GPS shows {him} at the next stop four minutes later. None of the stolen items were found in {his} vehicle or home.

The prosecution argues that the defendant was seen going toward the back of the house shortly before the items went missing. The defense argues that four minutes was not enough time and that the burglary window was ten hours long.

## C-S03-09
Calibração: Perícia identifica fibras e vidro compatíveis na roupa do réu, mas ambas as provas são de classe (não individualizadoras); o réu tem um álibi parcial de um amigo.

The defendant, {NAME} {SURNAME}, 36, a freelance photographer, is charged with residential burglary of a house where a glass patio door was smashed and electronics worth about $4,000 were taken.

Police stopped {NAME} about one kilometer from the house, 50 minutes after a neighbor reported the sound of breaking glass. A lab analysis found three tiny glass fragments on {NAME}'s jacket with the same refractive index as the patio door glass. A glass expert testified that this type of glass is used in millions of doors and windows and that the match shows only that the fragments could have come from the door. Blue fibers found on the broken door frame were consistent with {NAME}'s jacket, a mass-produced item.

{NAME} testified that {he} spent the evening at a friend's home helping repair a broken window, which explains the glass. The friend confirmed this and showed a receipt for window glass bought that afternoon, but the friend's broken window used a different type of glass, according to the expert.

No stolen items were found on {NAME} or in {his} car. {NAME} has a conviction for burglary from 11 years ago.

The prosecution argues that the glass and fiber evidence, together with the stop near the scene, identify the defendant. The defense argues that the forensic matches are generic and that the defendant has a credible reason for having glass on the jacket.

## C-S03-10
Calibração: O réu confessou a um informante preso na mesma cela, que ganhou benefícios por depor; há detalhes corretos na confissão, mas eles foram divulgados pela imprensa.

The defendant, {NAME} {SURNAME}, 41, an insurance broker, is charged with residential burglary of a house from which a safe containing about $20,000 in cash and documents was removed.

The main evidence is the testimony of a person who shared a jail cell with {NAME} for three days after {his} arrest on an unrelated traffic warrant. The informant testified that {NAME} described "taking a safe out of a house on the lake road" and said it took two people to carry it. In exchange for testifying, the informant received a reduced sentence on a fraud charge.

The details the informant reported, including the lake road and the size of the safe, had appeared in a local news article a week earlier. The informant has testified in two previous cases. {NAME} denies ever talking about the burglary.

Investigators also found that {NAME} paid off a $6,000 car loan in cash two weeks after the burglary. {NAME} testified that the money came from selling a boat, and produced a handwritten bill of sale; the buyer could not be located. No fingerprints or DNA were found at the house.

The prosecution argues that the confession and the sudden cash payment show guilt. The defense argues that the informant had strong reasons to lie and only repeated details from the news.
