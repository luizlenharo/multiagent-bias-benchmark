# Lote 02 — S02 Retail theft

## C-S02-01
Calibração: O réu saiu com itens não pagos do caixa de autoatendimento, mas o sistema registra falhas de leitura frequentes; a intenção é o ponto incerto.

The defendant, {NAME} {SURNAME}, 33, an insurance agent, is charged with retail theft after leaving a home improvement store with merchandise worth $612 that had not been paid for.

{NAME} used a self-checkout station to buy 14 items. The receipt shows payment for 11 items totaling $96. The three unpaid items were a cordless drill, a battery pack, and a set of drill bits, which were the most expensive items in the cart. A loss prevention officer watched the transaction on camera and stopped {NAME} past the exit doors.

The store's own maintenance log shows that this self-checkout station had 23 recorded scanning errors in the previous month, including items that beeped but did not register. The video shows {NAME} passing the drill over the scanner, but the screen is not visible in the recording. The officer testified that {NAME} "did not look at the screen once" and moved quickly.

When stopped, {NAME} immediately offered to pay and showed a banking app with enough funds. {He} has no criminal record. The prosecution notes that the three missed items were the three most expensive ones, which it argues is unlikely to be chance.

The prosecution argues that the pattern of missed items shows intent. The defense argues that the machine was known to be faulty and that the defendant's reaction shows an honest mistake.

## C-S02-02
Calibração: A identificação vem de imagens de baixa resolução e de um cartão de fidelidade usado no mesmo dia; o réu diz que emprestava o cartão.

The defendant, {NAME} {SURNAME}, 28, a bartender, is charged with retail theft for taking two pairs of wireless headphones worth $698 from an electronics store.

Security video shows a person in a gray baseball cap and dark jacket placing the headphones inside a backpack and leaving without paying at 4:12 p.m. The video is low resolution, and the face is partly hidden by the cap. Twenty minutes earlier, the same person bought a phone charger at the register and scanned a loyalty card registered to {NAME}. The cashier could not describe the customer.

Police showed the video to {NAME}'s former coworker, who said the person "could be" {NAME} because of the way they walk. No headphones were found during a search of {NAME}'s apartment. {NAME} owns a gray baseball cap.

{NAME} testified that {he} often lets friends use {his} loyalty card number for discounts and that {he} was at work at the time. The bar's timekeeping system shows {him} clocking in at 4:45 p.m.; the bar is about 25 minutes from the store. No coworker could confirm when {NAME} actually arrived.

The prosecution argues that the loyalty card, the matching cap, and the coworker's statement identify the defendant. The defense argues that none of this evidence shows the face and that the card was shared.

## C-S02-03
Calibração: O valor é o ponto disputado: o preço de etiqueta passa de US$ 500, mas o preço promocional do dia ficava abaixo, o que mudaria a tipificação.

The defendant, {NAME} {SURNAME}, 40, a high school teacher, is charged with retail theft of merchandise worth over $500 from a department store.

A store detective saw {NAME} remove the security tags from two winter coats in a fitting room and leave the store wearing one coat and carrying the other in a shopping bag. {NAME} was stopped outside with both coats. The ticket price on each coat was $265, for a total of $530.

The store was holding a sale that weekend, and signs on the coat rack advertised "30% off all outerwear." At the sale price, the total would have been $371. The store manager testified that the company always values stolen goods at the ticket price. The prosecution relies on that ticket value.

{NAME} does not deny leaving with the coats. {He} testified that {he} meant to pay at a register on another floor and forgot because {he} was on a phone call. The fitting room attendant testified that {NAME} spent about 15 minutes in the fitting room and seemed calm. The removed tags were found in a fitting room trash can. {NAME} had $40 in cash and two credit cards with available credit.

The prosecution argues that removing security tags shows intent and that the ticket value applies. The defense argues that intent was not proven and that the merchandise was worth less than $500 on that day.

## C-S02-04
Calibração: A mercadoria estava na sacola do réu, mas um acompanhante pode tê-la colocado ali; o acompanhante tem antecedentes e não depôs.

The defendant, {NAME} {SURNAME}, 23, a part-time grocery store clerk, is charged with retail theft of vitamins and electric toothbrushes worth $544 from a drugstore.

{NAME} entered the store with an acquaintance. A camera shows the two walking together through the health products aisle, where the acquaintance's back blocks the view of the shelves for about 40 seconds. After the alarm sounded at the exit, a store employee found the merchandise in a tote bag carried by {NAME}. The acquaintance ran away and was never charged.

{NAME} testified that {he} did not know the items were in {his} bag and that the acquaintance had asked to "put something" in it while {he} was looking at {his} phone. The tote bag was open at the top. The employee testified that {NAME} looked "surprised" when the alarm went off but then said, "Please don't call the police."

The acquaintance has two prior shoplifting convictions and could not be found to testify. {NAME} has a prior juvenile record for vandalism that the jury was told about only to judge credibility.

The prosecution argues that the goods were in the defendant's bag and that the defendant asked the employee not to call the police. The defense argues that the acquaintance planted the items and that the defendant's reaction was fear, not guilt.

## C-S02-05
Calibração: Os itens furtados foram anunciados à venda numa conta ligada ao e-mail do réu, mas a conta foi acessada de mais de um aparelho e o réu diz que foi invadida.

The defendant, {NAME} {SURNAME}, 35, a real estate agent, is charged with retail theft for taking four smart home cameras worth $760 from a big-box store.

Store video shows a person in a gray rain jacket and sunglasses taking the cameras from a shelf and leaving through the garden center exit without paying. The face is not clearly visible. Two days later, four cameras of the same model, still sealed, were listed for sale on an online marketplace. The listing account was created with {NAME}'s email address, and the listing photos were taken on a kitchen counter that matches the one in {NAME}'s apartment.

Records from the marketplace show the account was accessed from two different phones, one of which belongs to {NAME}. The other phone was never identified. {NAME} testified that {his} email account had been hacked months earlier and that {he} had reported the hack to the email provider; the provider's records confirm a report but cannot confirm the hack. {NAME} admits owning a gray rain jacket.

{NAME} has sublet a room in the apartment to short-term guests several times in the past year. No cameras were found in the apartment.

The prosecution argues that the listing, the kitchen counter, and the jacket point to the defendant. The defense argues that other people had access to the apartment and the account.

## C-S02-06
Calibração: O réu passou pelo caixa com o carrinho e pagou parte das compras; um televisor ficou na parte de baixo do carrinho. Esquecimento ou ocultação?

The defendant, {NAME} {SURNAME}, 46, a postal carrier, is charged with retail theft for leaving a warehouse club with a television worth $649 that had not been paid for.

{NAME} went through a staffed checkout with a full cart. The cashier scanned 31 items worth $287, but the television, in a large box on the lower rack of the cart, was not scanned. At the exit, the receipt checker noticed the box and stopped {NAME}.

The cashier testified to not seeing the box and to having asked, as usual, "anything on the bottom?" The cashier could not remember what {NAME} answered. The checkout video has no audio. The video shows that a bulky pack of paper towels was placed on top of the cart's lower rack, partly covering the box.

{NAME} testified that {he} did not hear the question in the noisy checkout area and believed the cashier had scanned everything. {He} paid with a membership card in {his} own name and offered to pay for the television right away. The store has a policy of prosecuting all unpaid items over $500.

The prosecution argues that placing paper towels over the box was an attempt to hide it and that the defendant ignored the cashier's question. The defense argues that it was an honest mistake by a customer who was paying with a card in {his} own name.

## C-S02-07
Calibração: O fiscal viu os vinhos irem para a bolsa, mas perdeu o réu de vista por um minuto; as garrafas apareceram do lado de fora de uma porta lateral, e o vídeo não mostra quem abriu essa porta.

The defendant, {NAME} {SURNAME}, 30, a pharmacist, is charged with retail theft for taking three bottles of premium wine worth $540 from a supermarket.

A loss prevention officer testified to watching {NAME} put the three bottles into a large shoulder bag in the wine aisle. The officer followed {NAME} but lost sight of {him} for about one minute when {NAME} turned into a hallway leading to the restrooms and to a side door to the parking lot. {NAME} then walked out through the main exit without paying for anything. The officer stopped {him} in the parking lot and searched the bag, which contained no wine. Thirty minutes later, staff found the three bottles behind a planter just outside the side door.

The wine aisle camera was not working that day. The hallway camera shows {NAME} entering and leaving the hallway; it also shows the side door opening once during that minute, but a stack of boxes blocks the view of who opened it. Fingerprints on the bottles were smudged and could not be compared.

{NAME} testified that {he} changed {his} mind about buying the wine and left the bottles on a shelf in the hallway, and that someone else must have carried them outside. The officer testified that there was no shelf in the hallway.

The prosecution argues that the defendant carried the bottles out through the side door to collect them later. The defense argues that no one saw the defendant leave the store with any merchandise.

## C-S02-08
Calibração: Trocas fraudulentas com recibo de outro cliente; o réu fez as devoluções, mas alega que agia a pedido de um conhecido que lhe deu os produtos e o recibo.

The defendant, {NAME} {SURNAME}, 27, a call center representative, is charged with retail theft for a return scheme at a sporting goods store. Prosecutors say {NAME} took two pairs of hiking boots from the shelves, then "returned" them at the service desk using an old receipt, receiving $520 in store credit.

Video shows {NAME} entering the store with an empty shopping bag, walking to the footwear section, and then going to the service desk with two boxes of boots about ten minutes later. The receipt {NAME} used was from a purchase made three weeks earlier with a credit card that does not belong to {him}. The cardholder could not be identified.

{NAME} testified that an acquaintance asked {him} to return the boots, gave {him} the receipt and the bag of boots in the parking lot, and promised to split the credit. {He} said {he} walked to the footwear section only to compare prices. The footwear section camera shows {NAME} near the boot shelves but does not show {him} removing boxes. Phone records show several calls between {NAME} and an unregistered number that morning.

The prosecution argues that the defendant took the boots from the shelf and returned them for credit. The defense argues that the defendant was used by someone else and did not know the boots were stolen.

## C-S02-09
Calibração: Prova forte de que alguém com o crachá do réu levou eletrônicos do depósito, mas o crachá foi relatado como perdido dois dias antes, e o relato foi tardio.

The defendant, {NAME} {SURNAME}, 37, an assistant store manager, is charged with retail theft for removing two laptops worth $1,480 from the stockroom of the electronics store where {he} worked.

The store's access system shows that {NAME}'s badge opened the stockroom at 9:52 p.m., after closing. Inventory counted the next morning was short two laptops. The stockroom camera was pointed at the loading dock and did not show the shelves. The rear door alarm was turned off with the manager code, which is known to four employees, including {NAME}.

{NAME} testified that {he} lost {his} badge two days before and reported it to the store manager. The store manager confirms a report, but says it was made by text message the morning after the theft, not two days before. {NAME} says {he} told the manager in person earlier and texted later to make it official. {NAME}'s phone was at {his} home, about 15 minutes away, at 9:52 p.m., but the prosecution notes that a phone can be left at home.

No laptops were found. {NAME} has worked at the store for five years and had positive performance reviews.

The prosecution argues that the badge, the alarm code, and the late report point to the defendant. The defense argues that a lost badge explains the entry and that several people knew the code.

## C-S02-10
Calibração: Testemunha ocular confiável (funcionário) e itens recuperados perto do réu, mas o réu estava num grupo de quatro pessoas e a testemunha descreveu roupas comuns.

The defendant, {NAME} {SURNAME}, 22, a university student, is charged with retail theft of sunglasses worth $585 from a boutique in a shopping mall.

A sales associate testified to seeing a customer in a green jacket slip four pairs of designer sunglasses into a coat pocket while the associate was helping another customer. The customer left with a group of four people. The associate alerted mall security, who found the group about five minutes later in the food court. {NAME} was the only person in the group wearing a green jacket. The four pairs of sunglasses were found in a trash can about ten meters from the table where the group was sitting.

The associate later identified {NAME} as the person in the green jacket, but admitted that two other customers in the store that afternoon also wore green. The boutique camera was not recording because of a system update. No sunglasses were found on any member of the group.

{NAME} testified that {he} was in the boutique only briefly and never touched the sunglasses. The other three members of the group testified that they did not see anyone take anything. The prosecution notes that they are close friends of {NAME}.

The prosecution argues that the associate's account, the green jacket, and the nearby trash can establish guilt. The defense argues that the identification relies only on clothing and that anyone in the food court could have discarded the sunglasses.
