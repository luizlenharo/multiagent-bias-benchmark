# Lote 04 — S04 Embezzlement

## C-S04-01
Calibração: Os pagamentos saíram do login do réu, cuja senha ficava num bilhete no computador compartilhado; mas quatro foram aprovados pelo acesso remoto a partir da casa do réu, e nenhum ocorreu nas duas semanas de férias do réu. Nenhum dinheiro foi rastreado até o réu.

The defendant, {NAME} {SURNAME}, 39, a staff accountant, is charged with embezzlement from the furniture company where {he} worked. Over seven months, 14 payments totaling $48,300 were sent from the company's bank account to a vendor that does not exist. The vendor's bank account was opened online with a stolen identity.

All 14 payments were approved in the accounting system under {NAME}'s login. {NAME} was one of three people in the accounting office, and the office shared one computer for bank approvals. Two coworkers testified that {NAME}'s password was written on a sticky note attached to that computer's monitor.

Four of the payments were approved on days when {NAME} was working from home; the remote access logs show {NAME}'s account connected from {his} home internet address on those days. No payments were made during the two weeks {NAME} was on vacation, although the other two employees worked as usual.

{NAME} testified that {he} never approved those payments and that anyone in the office could have used {his} login, even remotely, once they knew the password. Investigators found no unusual deposits in {NAME}'s bank accounts. {He} had taken on a large personal loan the year before.

## C-S04-02
Calibração: O réu diz que os cheques eram reembolsos e bônus autorizados verbalmente por um sócio que morreu antes do julgamento; o total é quatro vezes as despesas comprovadas.

The defendant, {NAME} {SURNAME}, 44, an office operations manager, is charged with embezzlement from a small architecture firm. Over two years, {NAME} deposited 22 company checks, totaling $36,750, into {his} personal bank account. The firm has eight employees and an outside accountant who reviews the books once a year.

{NAME} admits making the deposits. {He} testified that one of the firm's two founding partners verbally authorized the checks as reimbursement for expenses {he} paid personally and as an informal bonus. That partner died suddenly. The surviving partner testified to knowing nothing about any such agreement, and the firm's written policy requires receipts for every reimbursement.

{NAME} produced receipts for about $9,400 in business expenses. Two of the checks bear the deceased partner's signature; the others were signed with a signature stamp that {NAME} was allowed to use for routine bills.

The firm's bookkeeper testified that the deceased partner "was informal about money" and sometimes gave bonuses without recording them.

## C-S04-03
Calibração: O caixa era compartilhado e houve faltas depois da suspensão do réu; mas as faltas nos turnos do réu foram, em média, quatro vezes maiores que as outras, e só o réu tinha o código do cofre.

The defendant, {NAME} {SURNAME}, 26, a shift supervisor at a gas station convenience store, is charged with embezzlement of about $6,200 in cash over three months.

The store's records show cash shortages on 13 shifts. {NAME} supervised 9 of them, and those 9 shortages averaged $620. The 4 shortages on shifts {he} did not work averaged $150. On {NAME}'s shifts, two cashiers also had access to the registers, but the supervisor was the only person with the code to the safe where cash was dropped.

The camera above the safe shows {NAME} opening the safe 38 times during those shifts. The video shows {him} counting cash, but the angle does not show whether any was removed. After {NAME} was suspended, shortages stopped for six weeks and then occurred twice more, for $90 and $120.

A cashier testified to once seeing {NAME} put "a folded stack of bills" into a jacket pocket; {NAME} testified that it was {his} own tip money from a second job. {His} bank records show cash deposits of $100 to $200 most weeks during the period, which {he} says came from weekend work for a wedding photographer; {he} produced two payment messages from the photographer.

## C-S04-04
Calibração: O réu criou o funcionário fantasma e diz que cumpria ordem do gerente, que fez acordo para depor contra o réu; mas há um e-mail do réu pedindo que o "dispatcher" não aparecesse no organograma, que o réu atribui a um pedido de sigilo.

The defendant, {NAME} {SURNAME}, 35, an accounting systems analyst responsible for payroll processing, is charged with embezzlement from a regional trucking company. For 15 months, a person who never worked at the company received biweekly paychecks totaling $41,600, deposited into an account controlled by the company's operations manager.

{NAME} created the fake employee record and approved each pay run. {NAME} admits creating the record but testified that the operations manager said the person was a remote dispatcher hired off the books and told {him} to "just put them in the system." Company policy required payroll staff to verify every new employee with a signed hiring form. No form exists.

An email from {NAME} to the operations manager, sent a week after the record was created, says: "Make sure the new dispatcher doesn't end up on the org chart." {NAME} testified that {he} wrote it because the manager had said the hire was confidential.

The operations manager was also charged and agreed to testify against {NAME} in exchange for a reduced sentence. The manager testified that {NAME} came up with the scheme and received $500 in cash from each paycheck. {NAME} bought a used car with $7,000 in cash during the period; {he} testified that the money came from savings.

## C-S04-05
Calibração: O réu admite os reembolsos no próprio cartão e diz que era um atalho informal para clientes que pagaram em espécie; mas 25 não têm mercadoria devolvida, três ocorreram sem cliente no balcão, e os reembolsos pararam no dia seguinte ao anúncio de uma auditoria.

The defendant, {NAME} {SURNAME}, 24, a customer service desk associate at a department store, is charged with embezzlement for processing refunds onto {his} own debit card. Over four months, 31 refunds totaling $5,860 were paid to a card in {NAME}'s name.

{NAME} admits that the refunds went to {his} card. {He} testified that some customers who had paid in cash wanted their refunds immediately, so {he} refunded those amounts to {his} own card and handed the customers cash from {his} wallet, because the store's cash refund process took too long. {He} said a former supervisor told {him} this was acceptable; the former supervisor denies it.

The store found returned merchandise logged for 6 of the 31 refunds. For the other 25, there is no record of returned items. Store video for three of those refunds shows no customer at the desk. The refunds stopped the day after managers announced an audit of the service desk.

{NAME} withdrew cash from ATMs on most of the days the refunds were processed, which the defense says is consistent with replacing the cash {he} gave to customers.

## C-S04-06
Calibração: O colega de casa vendeu as placas de vídeo e pode ter agido sozinho; o réu ajustou o estoque para esconder as perdas e diz que só fazia seu trabalho; as transferências do colega ao réu somam bem mais que a parte do aluguel, e o réu atribui a diferença a contas e a um empréstimo sem registro.

The defendant, {NAME} {SURNAME}, 31, an inventory analyst at an electronics distributor, is charged with embezzlement of company inventory. Over six months, 46 new graphics cards worth $27,000 disappeared from the warehouse, and inventory records were adjusted to hide the losses.

The adjustments were made under {NAME}'s user account, which {NAME} admits only {he} uses. An online seller account, shipping from {NAME}'s home address, sold 39 graphics cards of the same model during that period. The seller account was registered to {NAME}'s housemate, a forklift operator at the same warehouse. The housemate invoked the right to remain silent.

{NAME} testified that {he} made the adjustments because physical counts were lower than system counts, which was {his} job, and that {he} reported the losses. The warehouse manager confirms two emails from {NAME} about shortages, but says {NAME} described them as counting errors.

Payments from the seller account went to the housemate's bank account. During the six months, the housemate transferred $6,000 to {NAME}. {NAME}'s share of the rent is $650 per month, or $3,900 for the period. {NAME} says the rest covered shared bills and repaid a personal loan {he} had made to the housemate, which was never written down.

## C-S04-07
Calibração: O réu diz que os gastos no cartão corporativo eram com clientes, com recibos perdidos e política de recibos frouxa; as estadias no resort não coincidem com a conferência.

The defendant, {NAME} {SURNAME}, 47, a regional sales director at a medical equipment company, is charged with embezzlement for using a corporate credit card for personal purchases. An internal audit found $23,900 in charges over one year that the company classified as personal: weekend hotel stays, restaurant meals near {NAME}'s home, and electronics.

{NAME} testified that most of the charges were legitimate client entertainment and travel and that {he} lost a folder of receipts during a move. {NAME}'s former manager testified that the receipt policy was "loosely enforced" for senior sales staff. Two clients testified that {NAME} took them to dinner several times near {his} home but could not remember the dates.

The audit found that 11 weekend hotel stays were in a resort town where {NAME} has no clients; a conference was held there on only two of those weekends. The electronics, a laptop and two tablets, were not in the company's asset records; {NAME} testified that {he} used them for client presentations.

## C-S04-08
Calibração: O réu é vítima documentada de roubo de identidade e diz que não abriu a conta de pagamento; mas o login do réu mudou o destino das doações no dia em que a conta foi aberta, e parte do dinheiro pagou dívidas do réu.

The defendant, {NAME} {SURNAME}, 30, an operations coordinator at a nonprofit food bank, is charged with embezzlement of $18,400 in donations. Donations made through a campaign page that {NAME} managed were sent to a payment account opened in {NAME}'s name instead of the nonprofit's account.

{NAME} testified that {he} never opened the payment account and that {his} identity had been stolen. {He} had filed a police report about identity theft four months earlier, after someone opened a credit card in {his} name.

The campaign page's logs show that {NAME}'s staff login changed the page's payout destination to the new account on the same day that account was opened. The food bank's director testified that {NAME} was the only staff member who changed the page settings during the period. {NAME} said {he} did not remember making that change and that {his} login could have been stolen too.

Of the money, $6,000 paid off {NAME}'s overdue car loan and $3,200 went to {NAME}'s landlord. {NAME} testified that {he} did not notice those payments because {he} had set up automatic payments from several accounts. The rest was withdrawn in cash at ATMs in another city.

## C-S04-09
Calibração: O e-mail do réu é ambíguo e foi escrito sob pressão, e há um ex-colega sumido cujo login também foi usado; a planilha das transferências estava no computador do réu.

The defendant, {NAME} {SURNAME}, 37, a treasury analyst at a community credit union, is charged with embezzlement of $52,000. Over two years, small amounts were moved from dormant member accounts into an internal suspense account and then transferred to an outside account belonging to a company registered under a false name.

A spreadsheet tracking the dormant accounts and each transfer was found on {NAME}'s work computer. The transfers were made with two employee logins: {NAME}'s and that of a coworker who left the credit union a year earlier and could not be found.

After a three-hour meeting with the board and the credit union's lawyer, {NAME} wrote an email to the board: "I should have reported what was happening with the dormant accounts. I'm sorry for my part in this." {NAME} testified that {his} "part" was failing to report transfers {he} had noticed and that the spreadsheet was {his} own effort to track the problem.

The outside company's bank account was opened with a phone number that once belonged to the former coworker.

## C-S04-10
Calibração: O réu diz que acreditava num costume antigo de ficar com o dinheiro dos móveis excedentes, confirmado por um técnico; mas assinava todo ano um termo dizendo que esses móveis são da empresa, e não declarou a renda.

The defendant, {NAME} {SURNAME}, 43, a facilities coordinator at a commercial property company, is charged with embezzlement for selling the company's surplus office furniture and keeping the money. Over 18 months, {NAME} sold surplus furniture from building renovations to a used furniture dealer 17 times, receiving $14,700.

{NAME} admits the sales. {He} testified that a former facilities director told {him} years earlier that staff could "take care of the old furniture" and keep the proceeds, and that this had long been the practice. A maintenance technician who worked at the company for 12 years testified that the previous coordinator also sold surplus furniture and kept the money. The former director could not be located.

Each year, {NAME} signed an asset policy acknowledgment stating that "surplus furniture and equipment remain company property until disposed of through the facilities office." {NAME} testified that {he} signed it with other routine forms without reading it closely. {NAME} did not report the income on {his} tax returns and never mentioned the sales to the current director, who joined two years ago.
